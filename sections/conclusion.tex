This study verified a conjugate heat transfer model of a simplified medical device geometry, built with the \texttt{chtMultiRegionFoam} solver in \texttt{OpenFOAM}, as the first step in a risk-informed credibility assessment following ASME V\&V 40.

Code verification used three analytical benchmarks, each isolating one part of the solver. Hagen--Poiseuille flow verified momentum transport, with an observed order of convergence of $p = 1.95$; steady radial conduction with internal heat generation verified the solid energy equation, with $p = 2.08$; and transient radial diffusion in a fluid at rest verified transient thermal transport and the transfer of the solid temperature across the fluid--solid interface, with $p$ between 2.15 and 2.47 over the times examined. These observed orders match or exceed the formal second-order accuracy of the spatial discretization.

Solution verification extended the analysis to coupled configurations without analytical solutions. The steady coupled pipe converged at $p = 1.90$ relative to a \texttt{StREEQ} extrapolation. For the transient simplified EDDS, co-refining the mesh and time step showed that spatial and temporal discretization errors interact: refining either one alone yielded reduced apparent orders ($p \approx 1.06$), because the error from the parameter held fixed persists. Co-refinement is therefore needed to estimate discretization error reliably in transient coupled simulations. Solver tolerances of $10^{-6}$ or tighter contributed negligible iterative error, and the interface energy imbalance remained of order $10^{-4}$ of the applied heat source at all resolutions, indicating that heat flux is conserved across the fluid--solid interface.

Because computational cost grows faster than accuracy improves, and does not vary monotonically with resolution, the discretization for subsequent studies was chosen from measured runtimes: the M3--T3 configuration gives a relative error of about 0.1\% in 24 minutes and is adopted for the validation studies.

For the medium--low risk context considered here, these results show that targeted simplification of the geometry allows verification rigor beyond minimal practice within practical computational limits. The simulation inputs, mesh scripts, and analysis scripts are publicly available, and a companion study will build on this foundation with validation and uncertainty quantification.
